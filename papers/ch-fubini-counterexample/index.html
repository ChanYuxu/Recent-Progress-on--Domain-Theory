The Fubini Problem for Continuous Valuations on dcpos: A Counterexample under the Continuum Hypothesis

Conditional counterexample · Choquet integration · Scott product topology

This explanatory article is AI-generated. Please check the manuscript, formal statements, and verification limits before relying on its conclusions.

Changing the order of integration is usually a routine step in probability. For continuous valuations on arbitrary dcpos, it is a substantive question. The manuscript discussed here constructs, under the continuum hypothesis, a Scott-open relation whose two iterated Choquet integrals are 1 and a positive number no larger than ε. The smaller value can be made arbitrarily close to zero, even though both valuations are probabilities and both inner-integral functions are Scott-continuous.

The result, with its hypothesis. Assume CH. For every 0 < ε < 1, there are dcpos P and L, continuous probability valuations μ and ρ, and W ∈ σ(P × L) such that

∫L ∫P 1W(C, V) dμ(C) dρ(V) = 1,

∫P ∫L 1W(C, V) dρ(V) dμ(C) = qε,

qε = 1 − ∏n ≥ 1(1 − ε2−n),   0 < qε ≤ ε < 1.

L is a complete lattice; ΣP is first-countable and sober; both Scott spaces are well-filtered. The two functions obtained after the inner integrations are Scott-continuous.

Which Fubini equation is at stake?

A dcpo is a partially ordered set in which every nonempty directed subset has a supremum. Its Scott opens are upper sets that detect directed suprema at some stage. A continuous valuation assigns mass to opens, is strict and modular, and preserves arbitrary directed unions. For a lower-semicontinuous nonnegative function, its Choquet integral is defined by the layer-cake formula

∫ f dν = ∫0∞ ν({x : f(x) > r}) dr.

In particular, the integral of the indicator of an open set U is ν(U). The domain-theoretic Fubini question asks whether the two orders agree for Scott-continuous functions on the coordinatewise ordered product of two dcpos.

The topology matters: σ(P × L) need not equal the ordinary product topology ΣP × ΣL. This is not a counterexample to classical Fubini–Tonelli on measurable product spaces. In this construction, W is genuinely open in the first topology and is not open in the second.

The broader context is probabilistic powerdomains. Jia, Lindenhovius, Mislove, and Zamdzhiev constructed commutative valuation submonads on DCPO in Commutative Monads for Probabilistic Programming Languages. Jia, Mislove, and Zamdzhiev subsequently introduced the central valuations monad. Centrality requires a valuation to commute with every partner valuation. The present manuscript gives a CH-conditional failure for the full continuous-valuation setting.

A source space with mass-one tails

Start with the unit circle 𝕋 and its normalized Lebesgue probability λ. Under CH, its Borel null sets and positive-measure compact sets can be enumerated as (Nγ) and (Kγ), indexed by γ < ω1. Recursively choose aγ in Kγ, avoiding all Nξ with ξ ≤ γ and all previously chosen points. Every stage is feasible: its forbidden set is a countable union of null sets, while Kγ has positive measure.

Put A = {aγ : γ < ω1}. Null Borel sets have countable, initial-segment-bounded traces on A; every positive-measure compact set meets A; and a Borel set whose trace on A is countable must be null. These are the three properties used by the later construction.

Refine the topology on A using basic opens (A ∩ O) ∖ Iβ, where O is ordinary open in 𝕋 and Iβ = {aγ : γ < β}. The resulting space is Hausdorff and first-countable. Its Borel probability mA has the actual trace identity mA(A ∩ B) = λ(B) for Borel B; the construction does not assume that A itself is Borel in the circle.

Restricting this measure to opens gives a continuous probability valuation α. Each tail Tβ = A ∖ Iβ has α-mass one. Yet every compact subset of the refined source is countable and has mass zero. Thus this source probability is not inner regular: the argument must not treat it as a Radon probability.

Random small opens, with a large tail attached

Let Ω = 𝕋ℕ carry the product probability ℙ. For n ≥ 1, choose an arc length ℓn = ε2−n and radius rn = ℓn/2. A sample s = (sn) determines the union of circle balls Us = ⋃n ≥ 1 B(sn, rn). Its pullback Rs to A has α-mass at most ε.

Now take L = 𝒪(A), ordered by inclusion, with its full Scott topology. The maps Fβ(s) = Tβ ∪ Rs are continuous Ω → ΣL, and every value has α-mass one. At any fixed point x = aγ, however, the tail stops including x once β > γ. The eventual probability of membership is then just the chance that the random balls hit x:

ℙ(x ∈ Rs) = 1 − ∏n ≥ 1(1 − ℓn) = qε.

Independence of the coordinates gives the product. The first ball gives qε ≥ ε/2 > 0, and the union bound gives qε ≤ ε. The contrast is already visible: every stage has full α-mass, while each fixed point eventually has only a small membership probability.

The difficult step: a continuous limit valuation

For a Scott-open test ℋ ⊆ L, consider the ordinary open events Eβ(ℋ) = Fβ−1(ℋ). They decrease with β. Since Ω is second-countable, each decreasing ω1-sequence of open events eventually becomes constant. Thus

ψ(ℋ) = ⋂β < ω1 Eβ(ℋ),   ρ(ℋ) = ℙ(ψ(ℋ))

defines an ordinary open event ψ(ℋ) and its probability. But eventwise stabilization alone is not enough: its stage depends on ℋ, and continuous valuations must preserve arbitrary directed unions, not merely increasing sequences.

The key lemma proves that, for every set-indexed family (ℋi), the difference ψ(⋃i ℋi) ∖ ⋃i ψ(ℋi) has probability zero. Its proof uses a compact positive-measure carrier, dense random incidence, and Baire category. One carrier stage is fixed independently of the test; later stages may depend on the test. It does not take an uncountable intersection of dense sets.

This null-defect lemma, together with directed continuity of the ordinary product probability on opens, proves that ρ is a continuous probability valuation on L. The proof is not an appeal to a general rule that decreasing infima of continuous valuations remain continuous.

From point membership to a Scott-open relation

The other factor is the Smyth dcpo P = 𝒬(A): the nonempty compact subsets of the refined source, ordered by reverse inclusion. For this source its full Scott topology equals the upper Vietoris topology. The manuscript proves that ΣP is first-countable and sober and that both ΣP and ΣL are well-filtered.

Push α forward along the continuous singleton map e(x) = {x} to obtain μ. Use the incidence relation

W = {(C, V) ∈ P × L : C ⊆ V}.

This relation is Scott open for the coordinatewise order product. Its two sections yield the Scott-continuous profiles f(V) = μ(□V) = α(V) and g(C) = ρ({V : C ⊆ V}). In particular, g({x}) = qε for every source point.

Why the two integrals differ

For 0 ≤ r < 1, the test ℋr = {V : α(V) > r} has ρ-mass one: every Fβ(s) has α-mass one. For r ≥ 1 the test is empty. Consequently, integrating over P first gives

∫L f(V) dρ(V) = ∫01 1 dr = 1.

In the opposite order, the singleton pushforward reduces the integral of g to the constant qε on the source:

∫P g(C) dμ(C) = ∫A g({x}) dα(x) = qε.

Both are genuine Choquet computations. Neither missing continuity of an inner integral nor an infinite value accounts for the discrepancy.

What the example establishes—and what it does not

Both μ and ρ are noncentral: each has the other as a counterpartner. The manuscript also derives noncommutativity of the usual subprobability continuous-valuation monad under CH. Only ΣP is proved first-countable; the theorem does not assert this for ΣL. No unconditional ZFC counterexample or independence theorem is claimed.

The role of CH is to construct the source while keeping all initial stages countable. Removing that hypothesis, finding a counterexample with both Scott spaces first-countable, and identifying stronger intrinsic conditions that force centrality remain questions stated in the manuscript.
