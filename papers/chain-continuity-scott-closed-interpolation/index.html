Chain Continuity and Scott-Closed Interpolation

Yuxu Chen · arXiv:2610.18692v1 · math.GN / math.LO

All content in this section is AI-generated. AI may make mistakes; please verify important information carefully.

The ordinary way-below relation tests approximation against directed sets. What remains true if we test only against chains? This paper shows that chain approximation still determines ordinary continuity on dcpos, but that its own interpolation law can fail. It then finds a different failure of interpolation when the tests are Scott-closed sets.

Main theorem. On every chain-continuous dcpo, ordinary way-below is the relational square of chain-below: x ≪ y exactly when there is z with x ≪C z ≪C y. Consequently the dcpo is continuous. In arXiv v1, the corresponding upper sections also form a basis for the Scott topology.

Why one composition matters

Chain-below tests fewer sets than ordinary way-below, so a single chain-below step can relate more pairs. The proof isolates a cofinality pattern that any noninterpolating chain-below pair must have. Two such failures cannot occur consecutively. That obstruction makes the relational square idempotent; its lower sections are directed and recover their endpoints as suprema. Scott openness of the corresponding upper sections then identifies the square with ordinary way-below.

The paper also singles out a class where the square is unnecessary. Every countably based continuous dcpo has chain-below equal to ordinary way-below and is strongly chain-continuous. The arXiv version states this as a separate proposition.

A strict example, and the limit of directed completeness

The square equality does not say that chain-below itself interpolates. A subalgebra lattice built from a locally finite Jónsson algebra of cardinality ℵ1 is a chain-continuous algebraic complete lattice, but its chain-below relation strictly contains ordinary way-below. This makes the square genuinely necessary, with no continuum-hypothesis assumption.

Directed completeness is also essential to the recovery theorem. A separate poset of cardinality ℵ1 is strongly chain-precontinuous under both the cut-based and existing-supremum conventions, yet fails directed predistributivity under both conventions.

Scott-closed tests can fail independently

The second part studies approximation tested by Scott-closed sets. On lower-set lattices, a finite-upper-bound condition characterizes the resulting beneath relation, and interpolation is equivalent to an extension property for directed lower sets. These descriptions turn two earlier interpolation questions into a concrete construction problem.

A countable height-two poset supplies the counterexample. Its lower-set lattice is countably based, completely distributive, and algebraic. There, chain-below already agrees with ordinary way-below; nevertheless, the Scott-closed beneath relation is incomparable with their common relation and does not interpolate. Thus this failure persists even after chain approximation has become exact.
