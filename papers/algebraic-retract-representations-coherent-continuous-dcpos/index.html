Algebraic retract representations of coherent continuous dcpos

Scott continuity · algebraic retracts · coherence · Lawson compactness

All content in this section is AI-generated. AI may make mistakes; please verify important information carefully.

A continuous dcpo is a Scott-continuous retract of an algebraic one. But the algebraic dcpo obtained from an arbitrary basis need not be coherent. This paper constructs a different algebraic representative that preserves coherence and, when present, Lawson compactness and a countable basis.

Theorem 1.1. For each nonempty coherent continuous dcpo D, there are Scott-continuous maps s: D → A and r: A → D with r ∘ s = idD. The dcpo A is coherent and algebraic, and each of its compact elements has only finitely many compact elements below it. If D is Lawson compact, A can be chosen Lawson compact as well.

From a basis to finite systems

Coherence controls intersections of compact saturated sets in the Scott topology. It does not say that the upper bounds of a finite family inside an arbitrary basis are generated by finitely many points of that basis. The proof therefore works with a poset of finite systems, ordered by restriction, instead of completing the original basis directly.

Each system p is valid on a Scott-open set Up. If two systems are valid at the same point, they have a common refinement there. A fixed finite family of refinements suffices: the union of their validity opens is exactly Up ∩ Uq. As a result, the systems valid at a point form an ideal. The section sends the point to this ideal, and the retraction takes the directed supremum of the approximants recorded by an ideal.

Compactness and countable bases

For a Lawson-compact D, Scott compactness gives a finite cover by validity opens. Restricting the system poset to the upward closure of the corresponding systems still gives a retraction and makes its ideal completion Lawson compact. If the original basis is countable, the finite systems, and hence the principal ideals that form the new compact basis, are countable too.

Two applications of the representation

Function spaces. If D is Lawson compact and continuous and R is an RB-domain, then [R → D] is Lawson compact and continuous, while [D → R] is an RB-domain. The proof obtains the relevant function spaces as retracts of algebraic function spaces and transfers their properties.

Prefinite approximation. A dcpo is Lawson compact and continuous exactly when its identity is the directed supremum of Scott-continuous maps below it that factor through prefinite algebraic dcpos. A prefinite algebraic dcpo need not be finite: its compact elements are generated from a finite set by iterating minimal upper bounds, with property M. The approximating family can be chosen as an increasing sequence exactly when the dcpo is countably based.
