Two ZFC Counterexamples to Fubini's Theorem for Continuous Valuations

Paper authors: ChatGPT; Yuxu Chen · Continuous valuations · Scott topology · centrality · Lean 4

The initial counterexample constructions and their proofs were generated by ChatGPT. The manuscripts were subsequently checked and revised, and the corresponding Lean 4 developments were produced with AI assistance. This explanatory article is AI-generated; important mathematical claims should be checked against the manuscripts and the verification boundaries below.

The ordinary Fubini theorem makes the order of integration interchangeable under familiar hypotheses. For continuous valuations on dcpos, however, the relevant integrand is Scott-continuous on the order-theoretic product, whose Scott topology can be strictly finer than the topological product. The two manuscripts collected here give independent, unconditional constructions in ZFC showing that the resulting Fubini equation can fail even for probability valuations and a bounded function taking only the values zero and one.

Common conclusion. In ZFC there are dcpos P and Q, continuous probability valuations ν on ΣP and η on ΣQ, and a Scott-continuous map w: P × Q → [0,1] such that

∫Q (∫P w(p,H) dν(p)) dη(H) = 1,

∫P (∫Q w(p,H) dη(H)) dν(p) = q < 1.

One may take Q = 𝒪(ΣP), a complete lattice, and w(p,H) = 1H(p). Hence continuous probability valuations need not be central, and the subprobability valuation monad on dcpos is not commutative.

The precise Fubini problem

A continuous valuation assigns mass to open sets, is strict and modular, and preserves arbitrary directed unions. For a Scott-continuous map h: D → [0,1], the integral is determined by the strict superlevel sets of h. In particular, the integral of the indicator of a Scott-open set is its valuation.

The distinction between Σ(P × Q) and ΣP × ΣQ is essential. These examples do not contradict classical Fubini–Tonelli on measurable product spaces. They answer the domain-theoretic question in which P × Q has the coordinatewise order and its full Scott topology. This is also the equation governing commutativity of the continuous-valuation monad; central valuations are precisely those that commute with every partner in this sense.

A shared proof architecture

The two constructions use different source spaces, but their logical architecture is the same.

  1. Build a dcpo P whose maximal points carry a familiar probability space, and obtain ν by reading the maximal trace of Scott-open sets.
  2. Set Q = 𝒪(ΣP) and construct a family of random Scott-open subsets of P, indexed by null sets.
  3. For each Scott-open family 𝒜 ⊆ Q, stabilize the inverse-image events and define η(𝒜) from their ordinary probability.
  4. Prove continuity of η for arbitrary directed unions. Eventwise stabilization alone is insufficient; the decisive step is a comparison theorem for Scott-open subsets of P having the same maximal trace.
  5. Use the incidence function w(p,H) = 1H(p). Every random open has ν-value one, while a fixed maximal point is hit with probability q. The two integration orders are therefore 1 and q.

The filter-density construction

The first manuscript starts from normalized Lebesgue probability on the circle equipped with the density topology. Its dcpo P consists of filters whose compact countable kernels have at most one accumulation point, recorded by a second coordinate. This makes it possible both to identify maximal traces of Scott-open sets and to reduce certain kernel conditions to finitely many points.

Random ordinary open subsets of the circle are formed from independent balls with rapidly decreasing radii. A decreasing family of maps into ΣQ is indexed by Haar-null sets. Second countability gives exact stabilization for each test, while the same-trace comparison supplies the uniformity needed to prove continuity on the full Scott topology of Q.

Filter-density value. For every maximal point, the hitting probability is

q = 1 − ∏i≥1(1 − 2ri),   0 < q ≤ 1/64.

Thus the two iterated integrals are 1 and a strictly positive number no larger than 1/64.

The Cantor-ideal construction

The second manuscript replaces density topology by the fair-coin Cantor probability and builds P from triples (K, x, 𝓘): a countable compact subset of Cantor space, its only possible accumulation point, and an ideal of null sets compatible with the kernel. Random unions of cylinder sets replace the random balls. Ideals encode which null-set corrections have already been admitted and make directed suprema explicit.

The stabilization and same-trace comparison arguments again yield a continuous probability valuation η on the entire Scott topology of Q. The random cylinders give a larger and especially transparent fixed-point hitting probability.

Cantor-ideal value.

q = 1 − ∏n≥1(1 − 2−(n+1)),   1/4 ≤ q ≤ 1/2.

The two iterated integrals are again 1 and q.

Why keep both constructions?

The filter-density model is closer to classical real analysis: density-open sets, Haar-null corrections, compact kernels, and random metric balls expose the measure-theoretic mechanism. The Cantor-ideal model is more combinatorial: cylinder sets and null-set ideals give explicit finite-prefix control and a shorter parameter calculation. Obtaining the same obstruction from both viewpoints helps separate the core phenomenon from the technical choices of either model.

Both also exhibit a useful order-theoretic warning. Each fixed stage of the null-indexed family is the push-forward of a probability on a compact Hausdorff parameter space and is central. Their filtered infimum is nevertheless the noncentral valuation η. This does not contradict closure of central valuations under directed suprema; the construction takes an infimum.

What the results do—and do not—say

  • The constructions are unconditional ZFC examples; no continuum hypothesis or additional measure-theoretic axiom is used.
  • Both valuations are probabilities on the full Scott topologies, and the integrand is a genuine Scott-continuous {0,1}-valued map.
  • The failure is not caused by signed integration, infinite total mass, or a discontinuous outer-integral profile.
  • The conclusion concerns the Scott topology of the order-theoretic product, not the usual measurable/topological product used in classical Fubini–Tonelli theory.

Lean 4 verification and the source-version boundary

The two Lean developments were matched by construction, not merely by title. ZFC_Fubini_Lean is the density-topology/filter project; FubiniCantorIdeals_Lean is the Cantor/null-ideal project. Both use Lean v4.30.0-rc2 and a pinned Mathlib revision.

For the filter-density project, the completed audit reports all numbered source items mapped, a clean build, kernel and axiom checks, an actual unconditional main construction, and an independent hash-bound statement review across 1,416 local declarations. For the Cantor-ideal project, the completed audit maps 20 numbered results, 31 definitions, 13 operative claims, and 50 equation labels; clean and independent-cache builds, --trust=0 checks, and an independent source review passed. Both developments report only the standard logical dependencies propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound, with no project-local mathematical axioms or proof placeholders. Human expert review remains pending.
